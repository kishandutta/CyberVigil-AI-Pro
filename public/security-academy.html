CYBER SAFETY & DEFENSE ACADEMY

Cybersecurity Awareness & Prevention Guidelines

Learn easy and practical ways to protect yourself from online fraud, fake links, and digital scams.

How to Identify Phishing Links

Fake links try to trick you into entering passwords, PINs, or banking details. Check for these warning signs before clicking:

  • Misspelled Brand Names: Scammers slightly modify letters in well-known websites (like goolge.com, fl1pkart.com, or amaz0n.in) hoping you won't notice.
  • Check the Real Ending Domain: In misleading addresses like login.paypal-support.com, the actual website is paypal-support.com, NOT the real PayPal.
  • Unusual Website Extensions: Be extra cautious with strange endings like .top, .xyz, .tk, .work often used for cheap scam sites.
  • Never Trust Shortened Mystery Links: If an unknown message sends links like bit.ly or tinyurl, always test it here before opening.

Common Email Scams

Scammers disguise emails to look like genuine banks, employers, or couriers to create panic or excitement. Watch for these red flags:

  • Urgent Bank Account Warnings: Messages saying "Your account will be suspended within 24 hours" or "Update KYC immediately" to pressure you into hasty actions.
  • Fake Lottery & Cash Rewards: Claims that you have won a lucky draw, gift coupon, or reward points for a contest you never entered are 100% scams.
  • False Job & Work-From-Home Offers: Emails offering high daily income for liking videos or doing simple reviews while asking for an advance fee.
  • Dangerous File Attachments: Never open email attachments ending with .exe, .bat, or disguised as fake bills (invoice.pdf.exe).

Password Safety Rules

Strong passwords act as your digital lock. Protect your accounts, emails, and money with these straightforward, non-technical habits:

  • Combine 3 to 4 Everyday Words: A passphrase of random common words (like coffee-blue-happy-guitar) is easier to remember and nearly impossible to guess.
  • Never Reuse the Same Password: Keep separate passwords for your bank, primary Gmail, and social media accounts so a single leak doesn't compromise everything.
  • Turn On 2-Step Verification (OTP): Always enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) with an authenticator app or phone OTP so no one can log in without your phone.
  • Use a Password Manager: Avoid writing passwords on sticky notes or unencrypted files; let your browser's built-in manager safely remember them for you.
PROACTIVE DEFENSE PILLARS

4 Golden Principles of Digital Safety

Easy and smart rules to keep your personal data and devices completely safe from online frauds.

Launch Threat Scanner

1. The "Pause & Verify" Habit

Always pause and think before clicking unknown links, sharing personal details, or responding to urgent money/prize messages.

2. Strict Credential Isolation

Never reuse your important passwords across different websites. If one site gets hacked, your other accounts remain safe.

3. Zero-Trust Link Policy

Treat every unexpected link with doubt. Always check the official web address before entering your login or banking info.

4. Unconditional 2-Step Defense

Always turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on your important accounts for an extra layer of security.
