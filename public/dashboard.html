How to Identify Phishing Links

Fake links try to trick you into entering passwords or card numbers. Here is how you can spot them in seconds:

  • Watch for Misspelled Brand Names: Scammers slightly modify letters in well-known sites, like writing goolge.com or fl1pkart.com.
  • Look at the Real Ending Name: In links like login.paypal-support.com, the actual website is paypal-support.com, NOT official PayPal.
  • Never Trust Shortened Mystery Links: If an unknown sender sends links starting with bit.ly or tinyurl, scan it here first before clicking.

Common Email & Message Scams

Scammers pretend to be banks, government officials, or delivery companies to make you panic. Watch for these signs:

  • Fake Threats & False Urgency: Any message saying "Your account will be suspended within 24 hours unless you click" is almost always a scam.
  • Fake Lottery & Prize Offers: Messages claiming you won a cash reward, free gift card, or smartphone for a contest you never entered are 100% fake.
  • Dangerous File Attachments: Never download unknown files ending in .exe, .bat, or disguised as fake bills and invoices.

Password Safety Rules

A strong password keeps your emails, photos, and money safe from hackers. Follow these three simple habits:

  • Combine 3 to 4 Everyday Words: A passphrase like coffee-blue-happy-guitar is much easier to remember and nearly impossible for hackers to guess.
  • Never Reuse the Same Password: Keep separate passwords for your bank, social media, and primary email accounts.
  • Turn on 2-Step Verification (OTP): Enable phone OTP or authenticator apps so no one can log in even if they know your password.
